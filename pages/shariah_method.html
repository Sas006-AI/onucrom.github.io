Shariah Screening Process

Transparent, consistent, and logically routed.

⚠️ A Note Before Anything Else:

Shariah compliance is not private property, and there is no single, universally agreed global standard for it. Different scholars, boards, and jurisdictions apply different — sometimes differing — thresholds and interpretations, each working in good faith from the same core sources. Onucrom is not a Shariah board. We do not issue fatwas, and we do not claim final authority over what is or isn't compliant for anyone's personal religious reliance.

What we do offer is a transparent, consistently-applied screening process, so you can see at a glance where something is likely to stand — and, just as importantly, see exactly why. Our rating is a directional signal, not a guarantee, and not a replacement for your own scholar's guidance on a matter of personal religious obligation.

1. The Core Principles (What we screen against)

Our screening methodology is rooted in identifying and isolating exposures to activities broadly recognized as non-compliant under Islamic commercial jurisprudence.

Riba (Interest / Usury)
Any guaranteed, fixed return on money lent. We screen out conventional debt instruments, fixed deposit schemes, and interest-bearing leverage structures.
Gharar (Excessive Uncertainty)
Contracts containing deception, unacceptable ambiguity, or undefined outcomes. We flag instruments lacking clear terms or relying on unknown future events.
Maysir (Gambling / Speculation)
Acquisition of wealth by chance rather than productive effort. We flag zero-sum derivative trading, betting structures, and pure speculative plays.
Prohibited Sectors (Haram)
Business activities explicitly forbidden, including: conventional banking/insurance, alcohol, pork, adult entertainment, and weapons manufacturing.

4. The Four-Step Independent Screening process (Path 2)

Our systematic approach to evaluating uncertified Lane A assets, drawing upon AAOIFI financial ratio standards.

1
Prohibited Exposure Filter
Equity/Business Only
We analyze the core business activity. Does the company operate in areas treated as impermissible (e.g., conventional banking, alcohol)? If exposure is minor/incidental rather than central, we record the percentage extent rather than applying an automatic failure, looking at both presence and materiality.
2
Financial Ratio Filter
Equity/Business Only
We apply AAOIFI-aligned ratio thresholds to the company's balance sheet (e.g., debt-to-assets, illiquid-to-total assets). Alongside the standard ratios, we ask one additional question: are the business's primary banking relationships with Islamic banks? This isn't a hard ratio threshold — it's a signal of the business's own orientation, treated as a useful supporting input, not a standalone pass/fail.
3
Instrument Filter
All Investable Assets
Separately from the business, we examine the investment vehicle itself. If a Halal business raises capital using a haram instrument (e.g., issuing an interest-bearing bond or guaranteeing a fixed return on equity), it is disqualified here.
4
Niyat (Intention) Filter
All Investable Assets
This step is about you, not the instrument. We ask a short question about your intention going in: are you approaching this as genuine holding/participation, or primarily chasing short-term trading gains? Your answer never changes the instrument's rating. What it does is generate a separate comment if your stated approach leans toward a pattern Shariah scholarship generally discourages — excessive short-term speculation — as a gentle flag, not a judgment, and entirely independent of whether the instrument itself passes or fails.
