Macro
1 Bangladesh faces a tougher test of its public finances from mid-2027, when the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank will start using two new measures to assess debt risks. The multilateral lenders will look at how much government revenue goes on interest payments. They will also measure how much the government must raise each year to cover its budget deficit and repay maturing loans, relative to the size of the economy.
2 In a policy pivot, the government will now prioritise developing bankable public-private partnership (PPP) projects to attract greater private investment in infrastructure development.
3 The government of Bangladesh and ADB launched PPP Monitor: Bangladesh to strengthen public–private partnerships (PPPs) and mobilize private investment in sustainable infrastructure and improved public services.

1 Bangladesh Bank has brought crisis-hit Padma Bank under a resolution process, paving the way for its liquidation or merger as the bank has become financially non-viable after years of mismanagement, loan irregularities and mounting losses.
2 Rooppur plant starting electricity feeding into the power-hungry national grid, after long years gone into its installation, supplying 350 MW of power.
3 The sudden postponement of Monday's Minimum Wage Board meeting for the RMG sector sparked intense labour unrest on Tuesday, disrupting operations at some 640 factories across major industrial zones.
4 Garment owners do not want any more wage boards once this year's new minimum wage structure is announced, seeking instead to replace the periodic, often volatile review process with a fixed formula for annual pay rises.
5 Bangladesh’s apparel export growth in 4MFY27 came almost entirely from basic garments, while higher-value products made little headway, underscoring the country’s continued dependence on low-value items.
6 The government has revised the spectrum renewal price for three mobile operators at BDT 212.38 Bn for 79.2 MHz in the 900 MHz, 1800 MHz and 2100 MHz bands over 20 years. However, the operators will have to pay an additional BDT 78.94 Bn in interest if they follow the instalment arrangement. They will not have to pay interest if they settle the entire amount within the first year.

1 DSE to redefine ATB role as bonds move to main board: The Dhaka Stock Exchange (DSE) will amend the rules tied to the Alternative Trading Board (ATB), redefining its purpose. Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission (BSEC) has asked the DSE on November 10 to take measures by the end of this month to transfer the bonds listed on the ATB to the exchange's main board.
2 SEML IBBL Shariah Fund unitholders approve open-end conversion: Unitholders of SEML IBBL Shariah Fund have approved a proposal to convert the fund from a closed-end to an open-end structure, although the change remains subject to regulatory clearance. 99.998% of unitholders present voted in favour of the proposal.
3 MJL Bangladesh develops 5-acre green, automated warehouse complex near Ctg Port: MJL Bangladesh PLC as well as its involvement in shipping, storage tank terminals and LPG, is seeking to diversify its business by tapping into growing demand for warehousing and logistics services.
4 MTB partners with Grameenphone, Robi for Bangla QR payment: Mutual Trust Bank PLC has entered into strategic partnerships with the country's leading telecommunications operators, Grameenphone Ltd and Robi Axiata PLC to introduce Bangla QR-based payment facilities across their respective customer service touchpoints.
5 BBS Cables swings to BDT 1.08 Bn loss as government orders dry up: BBS Cables PLC has plunged into a BDT 1.08 Bn net loss in FY26, underscoring a dramatic reversal of fortune driven by drying government tenders and macroeconomic headwinds.
6 Metro Spinning's BMRE delay drags on, losses mount: Metro Spinning Ltd has failed to resume production despite keeping its factory shut for more than three years to modernise and expand its facilities. The company has now sought another year to complete the project, while its losses continue to mount and net asset value per share declines.
